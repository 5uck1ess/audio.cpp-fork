"The lighthouse stood at the very end of the long grey spit of land, and for forty years it had belonged to one man."
"Every evening, a little before the sun touched the water, Tobias climbed the hundred and twelve steps to the lamp room, wiped the great lens with a soft cloth, and waited for the light to be needed."
"He did not mind the climb. He did not mind the wind, either, though it found every gap in the old stone and sang through them all winter long."
"What he minded, if he minded anything, was the quiet that came after midnight, when the fishing boats had gone home and the sea went flat and black and seemed to be listening."
"On the night the storm came, he knew it before the barometer did. The gulls had gone silent that afternoon, and the air tasted of metal."
"By nine o'clock the rain was hammering the glass in long, angry sheets, and the waves had begun to throw themselves against the rocks with a sound like distant cannon fire."
"Tobias checked the lamp, checked the fuel, and checked the lamp again."
"Then he sat down in his worn wooden chair, folded his hands in his lap, and watched the beam sweep out over the churning water, once every ten seconds, steady as a heartbeat."
"Somewhere past eleven, he saw it: a small yellow light, low on the waves, rising and falling and not moving away. A boat, and a small one, much too close to the reef."
"He stood up so quickly that the chair fell over behind him."
"There was a horn on the gallery, an old brass thing he had not sounded in years, and he hauled it up and blew it again and again until his lungs burned."
"The little light turned, slowly, hesitantly, and began to crawl toward the channel he had marked with the red lamp."
"He kept sounding the horn until the boat slid safely behind the breakwater, and only then did he let his arms fall."
"In the morning, a young woman climbed the hundred and twelve steps with a flask of coffee and a face that was still pale from the night. She did not say much."
"She set the flask on the table, looked out at the calm, glittering sea, and then looked at him. Thank you, she said, for not letting the light go out."
"Tobias nodded, because he had never been good with words, and poured them both a cup. Outside, the gulls were back, and the water was as blue and innocent as if nothing had happened at all."
